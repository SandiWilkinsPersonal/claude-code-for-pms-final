What happened with release 4.2, and how we stop it happening again

A report from the Dispatch PM, October 2026. Rook Industries is a fictional course scenario. Every number comes from the course data, and anything still unproven is marked as a hypothesis.

The short version

Release 4.2 shipped clean. Then the phones went quiet.

3×

Callout support tickets went from 5–8 a week to 20–32 and stayed there.

29%

of pings were missed in the first five days (12–16 Aug). Before the release it was 2.3%.

4 of 16

responders almost stopped getting pings: from 70–86 each before, to about one a week by late August.

Most likely cause (a hypothesis; the code we have is a simplified copy and production is unconfirmed): a missed ping costs a responder as much as turning one down, nothing ever earns the points back, and the ping wait was cut from 90 to 60 seconds in the same release.
What shipped on 12 August

Three changes in one release, so the data can't separate them

Change 1

Proximity counts for more

Travel time up from 0.45 to 0.60. Recent acceptance down from 0.40 to 0.25.

Change 2

Ping wait cut

How long a ping stays live before it counts as missed: 90 s down to 60 s, the same for everyone.

Change 3

Filters, plus three fixes

Console filters now persist between sessions. Three small defect fixes. Support tickets about this are noise.

Nothing written down explains why the wait went to 60 s or why the weights moved. Release notes went to handlers; I found no notice to responders.

Module 4 · X-ray vision

In the code, only a "yes" adds points back

The prompt I wrote
Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.
What it surfaced

A miss costs 0.12, the same as a turn-down. A "yes" earns 0.08, and it is the only thing that adds points. Nothing decays, resets, or can be fixed by hand. A quiet responder is only pinged as a fallback, and a fallback miss digs them deeper.

Caution: dispatch-routing is a simplified copy with in-memory scores. It shows how the logic can behave, not what production does. Only Wen Li, who built it, can confirm.
What I can't yet prove

The honest gaps

Timer or score?

The 60 s wait and the new weights shipped together, and the pings table has no response time, only callout, responder, time sent, and outcome. I can't tell which one pushed these four out.

Vesper doesn't fit

In the code, Vesper's score was still about 0.8 on 17 Aug, while pings had already fallen from 12 to 5 that week. The score alone doesn't explain him.

Nobody has said

The engineering manager asked on 14 Aug whether the new weighting should treat people already turning jobs down differently. It is still unanswered. The data ends 6–7 Sep; nothing is known after.

What the fix is

A way back for quiet responders, not a quiet setting change

What changes for them

  • A responder who stops getting pings is brought back into the order after a set period.
  • A miss costs less than a deliberate turn-down.
  • No available responder goes more than N days without a ping.
  • A returner who keeps missing is flagged to their handler and Support, not quietly dropped again.
  • Handlers see in plain words how their responder is doing, and can tell them something true.

What it deliberately doesn't do

  • It is not a setting, and handlers configure nothing.
  • It doesn't revert 4.2.
  • It doesn't make turning jobs down cheaper.
  • It doesn't fix "gone before I could answer", which is the 60 s wait.
  • It doesn't guarantee anyone a share of callouts, and it never touches legal identity.

The hard test is Vesper: the brief's pass/fail is that, replaying the real pings, Vesper is back to a stated number of pings a week within two weeks. If he isn't, the design isn't done.
